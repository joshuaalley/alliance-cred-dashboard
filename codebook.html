What the data measure

How credible was an alliance commitment? Each estimate answers that for one commitment in one year: how confident the state relying on the commitment could be that its ally would support it in a war against common adversaries. The estimate is a latent credibility score, α, from a measurement model that combines expert survey ratings with ratings from a large language model (LLM).

This release covers 234 alliances in the ATOP data (version 5.1), 1815–2023: 440 directed series and 7,588 alliance-years.

Structure

One row per alliance × source × recipient × year, for each year from the alliance’s start to its end in ATOP. The key is alliance_id, cred_source, cred_recip, year.

Variable Type Description
estimate_id integer Row number within this release. Not stable across releases.
release text Release identifier, here cred-v2026.1.
alliance_id integer ATOP alliance identifier (atopid).
cred_source text Whose commitment is assessed: a member state, or the groups partners and proteges (see below).
cred_recip text Who relies on that commitment: a member state, partners or proteges.
year integer Calendar year.
irt_median number Posterior median of α, the headline estimate. Higher means more credible.
irt_mean number Posterior mean of α.
irt_sd number Posterior standard deviation of α.
irt_mad number Posterior median absolute deviation of α, scaled to be comparable to a standard deviation.
irt_q5, irt_q95 number 5th and 95th posterior percentiles: the 90% credible interval.
irt_q25, irt_q75 number 25th and 75th posterior percentiles: the 50% credible interval.
n_ratings integer Ratings behind this alliance-year: expert raters plus LLM raters (one per LLM provider, at most two).
n_human integer Expert raters behind this alliance-year.
confidence_tier text gold: two or more experts; silver: one expert; bronze: LLM ratings only.
model_version text Provenance: LLM / LLM run / measurement model.
vintage_date date Date of the model fit that produced this release.

Sources and recipients

What a series measures depends on the kind of alliance. The survey asked how sure the recipient was that it could expect support from the source in war against common adversaries.

Alliance type Series (source → recipient) Meaning
Bilateral A → B, and B → A How credible A’s commitment was to B, and B’s to A.
Trilateral partners → each member How credible the other two members’ commitments were to that member.
Multilateral, symmetric partners → partners How sure members were of support from at least one partner.
Multilateral, with a leading state leader → proteges How credible the leader’s commitment was to the other members.
partners → partners How sure members were of support from at least one partner.
proteges → leader How credible the other members’ commitments were to the leader. Not asked in the expert survey: LLM-only and exploratory.
Wartime coalition partners → partners Cohesion rather than support: how sure members were that the others would keep fighting for shared war aims.

The expert and LLM ratings use the survey’s four levels: (1) alliance in name only, no expectation of support; (2) low credibility, unsure of support; (3) moderate credibility, fairly sure; (4) high credibility, very sure. Coalitions use the same four levels for cohesion.

Confidence tiers

Tiers record expert coverage. A gold or silver alliance-year has expert ratings behind it; a bronze one rests on the LLM alone. Gold intervals are only modestly narrower than bronze ones, so gold means less uncertain, not certain. The dashboard’s alliance table shows each alliance’s best tier across its series.

Reading the estimates

  • The scale. α is a latent scale with no natural zero or units; only order and distance matter. In this release irt_median runs from −13.0 to 13.9, and the middle half of alliance-years lie between −2.2 and 3.4.
  • Use the intervals. The typical 90% interval is about 6.6 units wide. The measure separates clearly different alliances, not near neighbors.
  • One release at a time. Each release re-estimates the scale, so values from different releases are not comparable. Cite the release you used.

How the estimates are made

  • Expert survey. Experts rated alliances on the four-level scale, marking the years in which credibility changed: 71 submissions across two survey waves, covering 46 alliances.
  • LLM ratings. An open-weight LLM answered the same questions for every alliance, five runs on each of two providers. Where a member state’s own diplomatic records are published (mostly great-power alliances), the prompts included retrieved passages from collections such as Foreign Relations of the United States, the British and French diplomatic documents, Die Grosse Politik, and the Wilson Center Digital Archive. The model and run are given in model_version.
  • Measurement model. A Bayesian ordinal item-response model treats each expert and each LLM provider as a rater with its own severity. The LLM gets its own discrimination, estimated relative to the experts, so it counts for less where it is less informative. Estimates are posterior summaries from four chains of 1,000 draws.

Releases

New releases are planned annually and add the expert contributions accepted since the last one.

  • cred-v2026.1 (fit 2026-10-02, current). The LLM changed from Llama 4 Scout to gpt-oss-120b, along with updates to the prompts and retrieval; the expert data, measurement model and coverage are unchanged. Estimates changed substantially: series levels correlate 0.42 with cred-v2026.0. Agreement with the expert ratings rose.
  • cred-v2026.0 (fit 2026-07-03). First, preliminary release (Llama 4 Scout).

Contributing and contact

Experts can add ratings for any alliance through the contribution form. Every submission is reviewed before it enters the revised measure in the next annual update. Questions: Joshua_Alley@baylor.edu.
